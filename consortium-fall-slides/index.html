A tutor in every course

An AI tutor, and nothing else changed

Minje Park
HKU
What he did
He gave his students an AI tutor that knows his course, named it Panda, and showed them how to use it.
One thing he learned
Students used it much more once he showed them, in class, what to ask.
A tutor in every course

AI tutors in a core course of 1,300 students

Mi Kyong Newsom & Jane Jiang
Ohio State
What they did
They gave every student two AI tutors for exam preparation, and a third one for the novel The Goal.
One thing they learned
When the AI only asked questions, students pasted them into another AI. Rebuilt as a case study, it worked.

Characters students talk to

Her own AI case for the operations core

Pnina Feldman
UVA Darden
What she did
She wrote an AI case for her course: students interview characters to solve a problem that draws on the whole course.
One thing she learned
She could write it herself, with AI. No developer, no publisher.
Characters students talk to

Quizzes turned into mock job interviews

Zonghao Yang
Stevens
What he did
Instead of a multiple-choice quiz, an AI interviewer asks each student ten questions on the course, then gives personal feedback.
One thing he learned
Students only use a course AI if it offers something ChatGPT does not.
Characters students talk to

A consulting exercise with three AI characters

Christian Blanco
Ohio State
What he did
Students play consultants at an airport. Three AI characters each hold part of the data, and students must interview all three.
One thing he learned
Students want to use AI in class the way they will use it at work.

Students supervising AI

Students manage the AI like an intern

Wenchang Zhang
Indiana Kelley
What he did
In his data competition, students plan the analysis, give the AI one step at a time, and check what comes back.
One thing he learned
Results improved a lot: ten teams beat the course record. But some students felt they learned less.
Students supervising AI

Students specify and check, AI writes the code

Rob Bray
Northwestern Kellogg
together with Robbie Sanders
What they did
Students split a data analysis into small steps, describe each step in plain English, let the AI write the code, then design a test for each step.
One thing they learned
Students like having a real role: deciding what to do, and proving it is correct.
Students supervising AI

MBAs build their own AI tools

Ben Collier
Carnegie Mellon Tepper
What he did
In a seven-week course, MBA students program AI agents that analyze text and images, then build a project of their own.
One thing he learned
The hard part is now grading: how do you evaluate code that AI wrote?
Students supervising AI

A data course rebuilt around AI

Jun Li
Michigan Ross
What she did
She stopped teaching coding in class. AI writes the code, and class time goes to real business problems.
One thing she learned
Students cannot judge code they never learned to write.

A small interactive app for each chapter

Blair Flicker
South Carolina
What he did
He asks AI to build simple web apps where students play with a concept and see every calculation. Each takes about 20 minutes.
One thing he learned
He can add AI-built material without changing any assignment or exam.
